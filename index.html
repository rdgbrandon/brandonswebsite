01 About

A question is not just a bag of tokens, and a market is not a flat vector. Both have structure you can recover before the model starts predicting, and recovering it is what makes the prediction explainable.

That idea runs through both of my papers. In CondVQA, an LLM splits a conditional question into its condition and its actual question, so a vision model only ever looks at the frames the condition selects, instead of averaging over the whole video. In the fuzzy cognitive map work, a signed causal graph over economic, technical and sentiment concepts adapts its edges online, so a forecast can be traced back to the specific causal links that produced it.

I finished a B.S. in Computer Science and a Mathematics minor at Florida International University in August 2025 as a Presidential Scholar and Honors College student, then finished an M.S. in Data Science & Artificial Intelligence through FIU's accelerated pathway at 21, and I am now a Ph.D. student at the University of Florida. Outside the lab I play chess, lift, run intramural basketball and football, and keep a running supply of tricky math problems.

Online-Adaptive Fuzzy Cognitive Maps for Neuro-symbolic Financial Forecasting

IEEE International Conference on Machine Learning and Applications (ICMLA), 2026
Dual FCM pipelines · LSTM ensemble · NASDAQ Composite, 2020–2025

Financial markets shift regime faster than a fixed model can follow, and the models that follow them best are the hardest to interrogate. This work pairs Fuzzy Cognitive Maps, directed graphs whose signed edges are the explanation, with an LSTM and live news sentiment. Two parallel FCM pipelines (Yahoo Finance and Polygon/Google) adapt their causal edges online through an error-driven Hebbian rule, with Loughran–McDonald lexicon sentiment injected as an exogenous bias. A second, explanatory framework grows a map outward from a seed concept using peer-reviewed literature and LLM-proposed links, every edge carrying a citation a human can check.

CondVQA: A Benchmark for Conditional Visual Question Answering in Video Understanding

Brandon Rodriguez, Yanzhao Wu  ·  Florida International University, 2026
Master's thesis, advised by Dr. Yanzhao Wu · manuscript in NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks format

Most video QA models sample frames uniformly and hope the relevant ones are in there. CondVQA asks a different question first: what is this question actually conditioned on? An LLM backbone classifies each query as SIMPLE or CONDITIONAL and, when conditional, splits it. "When you see a lawn mower, what color is it?" becomes the condition Is there a lawn mower? and the question What color is it? The condition is scored against every frame, a subset K ⊆ N survives, and only that subset answers the question. It ships with a new 300-video annotated dataset built from the PE Video corpus, plus integrated-gradient attributions for sanity-checking what the vision model actually looked at.
